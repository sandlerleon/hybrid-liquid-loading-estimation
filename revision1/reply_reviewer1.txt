R1.1. Novelty: emphasize the integrated estimation and decision framework as the principal contribution
Response: Accepted. Section 1.3 now states that the principal contribution is the integrated estimation-and-decision framework and not any single component: the thermodynamic flash, the diagnostic regression and least-squares fusion are established, and what is added is their combination under an uncertainty budget that includes carryover, a one-sided damping rule, and a covariance-aware consistency test that decides whether a correction is applied. The list of contributions in Section 1.4 now begins with the framework, and the Abstract is reorganized around it.

R1.2. Fusion benefits: clarify when fusion outperforms individual estimators; the diagnostic-only case has a narrower baseline 95% error band
Response: You are right, and the manuscript now says so directly. In the reference case the diagnostic estimator alone (±3.99%) is narrower than every fused configuration of Table 3, including the full method (±4.26%); the Abstract, Section 5.2 and the Conclusions state this. The conditions under which fusion does outperform it are now mapped with common random numbers: when carryover occupies less than about 16% of operating time (Fig. 10b); when the diagnostic estimator is poorer than assumed, for example 30% or more instead of 20% (Fig. 10a, d); and when the diagnostic estimator carries a persistent positive bias at one site (Fig. 10c). Section 5.10 adds a comparison with alternative weighting rules, and shows that a one-sided hard switch is narrower than the diagnostic estimator alone at all prevalences examined, while the rule of Eq. (12) is better only when carryover is rare.

R1.4. Algorithm: specify how weights and uncertainty are recalculated after asymmetric damping.
Response: Section 3.5.1 (new) gives the procedure. The damping factor multiplies the generalized-least-squares weight of the thermodynamic estimate (Eq. 12), the weights are renormalized (Eq. 13), and the fused value and its uncertainty are recomputed from the renormalized weights, with the covariance term and the Birge factor (Eq. 14). Steps 6 and 7 of Algorithm 1 are updated to match. Section 3.5.1 also gives the justification of the one-sided form and identifies the damping factor as the Cauchy influence weight of iteratively reweighted least squares.

R1.5. Uncertainty: clarify carryover scaling, bias treatment, and conversion of diagnostic intervals to standard uncertainty
Response: Three paragraphs address these in turn. Carryover scaling (Section 3.5): the carryover fraction is a relative error of the thermodynamic estimate, so u_carry = 17.5% is applied as a relative standard uncertainty, and the allowance treats carryover as zero-mean scatter although its mean is 9.4%; the mean shift is handled by the damping rule. Bias treatment (Sections 3.3.1 and 4.2): the transfer bias is a spread across installations, drawn once per realization, and a persistent bias at one site is evaluated separately (Fig. 10c). Conversion of intervals (Section 3.3.1): u₂ = (q0.95 − q0.05)/(2 × 1.645) for approximately normal residuals, so the assumed 20% corresponds to a calibrated 90% interval of ±33%; non-normal residuals are treated with the coverage factor of the observed distribution.
Change: Sections 3.3.1, 3.5, 4.2, 5.11.

R1.6. Implementation: state the minimum diagnostic signals and requirements when downstream tapping is unavailable
Response: Section 6.2 now has a paragraph on the minimum signal set. For the orifice case: line pressure, temperature, differential pressure and indicated rate, the composition, and the high-rate differential-pressure signal; the permanent pressure loss ratio needs a downstream tapping. Without it the feature vector loses its recovery term and the declared uncertainty of the diagnostic estimator must be widened; the size of the widening can be known only from a fit. As an indication, if the actual relative uncertainty were 30% instead of 20% the full method would reach ±5.48% against ±5.71% for the diagnostic estimator alone (Fig. 10a). Without the high-rate differential-pressure signal the method is not supported.
Change: Section 6.2 (new paragraph); Table 10.
